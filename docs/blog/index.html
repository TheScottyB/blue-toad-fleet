450 Photos, One Shopkeeper, and a Friday 8 PM Deadline

Building an agent fleet on Google Cloud for a rural estate auction 2.3 miles up the road — one that still publishes its catalog as a wall of unlabelled snapshots.

By Scott Beilfuss · Richmond General, Richmond, Illinois

Disclosure: I created this piece of content for the purposes of entering the All Things Agentic Hackathon. It describes Blue Toad Fleet, my submission to that hackathon, and it is published publicly for that purpose.

The shop and the sale

I run Richmond General, a one-person heritage resale shop in Richmond, Illinois. Two and a third miles north, across the Wisconsin line in Genoa City, Blue Toad Auctions runs an estate sale roughly every two weeks. It is a five-minute drive. On a decent day it is a fifty-three minute walk.

Blue Toad is not a modern online auction. There is no bidding app, no lot-by-lot catalog, no filterable inventory. Every cycle, the sale is published as a single page of somewhere around 450 photographs of a pole barn full of estate goods — unlabelled, unnumbered, in whatever order the auctioneer walked the room — plus a list of SEO keywords. Absentee bids are real and welcome, by email, up to 8:00 PM the Friday night before. After that the channel closes until the sale is over.

So the constraint is not access, and it never was capital. The sale is up the road and I go when I can. The constraint is what every cycle costs before a single bid exists: 450 photographs reviewed by hand, obscure makers identified, comps researched, margin math worked, and a priced sheet assembled — every two weeks, by one person who is also running the counter.

That is the actual reason the published absentee channel has never once been used. Not distance. Preparing it by hand costs more than it returns.

What is checked, and what is not

The money path is deterministic: mechanic parsing, unit exposure, the fifteen-percent absentee fee, bid increments, budget allocation, workbook, and clerk-facing email all reconcile through one decision object. BT-002 is the concrete boundary case: a written “times three” ruling turns a $25 per-unit ceiling into $75 committed and $86.25 all-in.

The old July comparison workbook is quarantined because its legacy totals, joins, and synthetic comparison path are not auditable enough for submission evidence. The August fixture is also labeled historical until a fresh cycle resolves every allocated-lot question and seals one artifact manifest. The publisher now refuses to blur either boundary.

What the fleet actually does

Blue Toad Fleet turns that uncataloged photo wall into a priced, positive-expected-value absentee sheet. Six mechanisms do the work, and each exists because a naive version of this failed in a specific, expensive way:

  • Evidence-gated spatial groupingCapture order and captions provide the conservative baseline; reviewed similarity edges can join repeat views. A physical map appears only when a manifest-bound spatial sidecar proves the zones. Without it, the Gate says walk-order grouping.
  • Container Lot DecompositionA bounded pass lists visible contents. An alpha affects price only when its mark is observed and no mark question remains open; otherwise the lot keeps its bulk floor.
  • The Honest Refusal RuleOn an unmarked piece of pottery with no verifiable comp, the fleet emits NO EXTERNAL COMP — human pricing required rather than inventing a number. Refusing to guess is the safety feature, not a gap.
  • The Choice-Lot GuardCountry auctioneers sell groups as “choice” or “times the money.” Unknown mechanics refuse; a written ruling sets the unit exposure; and one clerk directive carries the same amount through the sheet and email.
  • Bounded Curator ChallengeA challenge exists only when a typed standing rule conflicts with fresh, lot-matched evidence. The model may phrase the conflict, but it may not add market claims or tell the operator to buy.
  • Deterministic Bid MathModel output stops at identification and estimation. Turning an estimate into an authorized number is pure, unit-tested Python — margin target, condition discount, the house's $5 bid increment, the 15% fee. Auditable, and the same every run.

Built on Google Cloud

Vision and reasoning run on Vertex AI — Gemini 3.6 Flash for the heavy multimodal passes, lighter models for triage, routed by how much a given photo is worth thinking about. The gate console and sourcing API run on Cloud Run. The pipeline now records per-call tokens, latency, retries, fallbacks, errors, rate snapshots, and measured cost; a fresh corpus run is required before publishing a full-cycle cost or speed.

The repository is public and MIT-licensed, with a four-command local demo that needs no Google Cloud account, no OAuth, and no keys. The release report records the current test counts instead of this article freezing a number that immediately goes stale.

What it does not do

Worth stating plainly, because a project that only lists its wins is not describing software. The fleet does not fetch the auction site — ingestion is a sanctioned gallery drop, not a scraper pointed at anyone. Comps are incomplete, so real lots still route to human pricing. The current August fixture is not release-eligible, the automated path does not transmit a bid, and the checked-in video predates the evidence-sealed media workflow.

Velocity to distill the information. Collaboration on the judgment.

That is the whole thesis. The agent's job is to make 450 photographs comprehensible before Friday at 8. Deciding what is actually worth owning is still mine.
